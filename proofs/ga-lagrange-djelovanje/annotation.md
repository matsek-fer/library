A second, genuinely different proof of Lagrange's theorem: the subgroup
$H$ acts on the underlying set of $G$ by $h \cdot g = gh^{-1}$, and the
theorem falls out of two general facts about actions — orbits partition
the set, and a free action has all orbits of size $|H|$. The coset
bookkeeping of the classical proof (equivalence relation, translation
bijection) is revealed as an instance of general orbit machinery: the
orbits are exactly the left cosets, but the count never uses that
identification. Tests/teaches: checking action axioms when the formula
needs an inverse to make right multiplication a left action, the orbit
relation as an equivalence relation, freeness (trivial stabilizers) via
cancellation, and the bijection $h \mapsto h \cdot g$ from freeness —
optionally phrased through orbit–stabilizer. Instantiates the principle
that a free action of $H$ on a finite set forces $|H|$ to divide the
set's size, of which Lagrange is the self-action special case; the same
template later proves Cauchy's theorem and the necklace proof of
Fermat's little theorem. Deliberately paired with the coset-partition
proof (proof/ga-lagrange-particija) to answer requests for two distinct
proofs of Lagrange: same theorem, different conceptual engine.
Difficulty 2 — needs the definition of an action but nothing beyond it.
