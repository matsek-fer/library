The classical coset-partition proof of Lagrange's theorem, written for a
reader who has just met the definition of a coset. Structure: define the
equivalence relation $x \sim y \iff x^{-1}y \in H$, check reflexivity,
symmetry and transitivity directly from the subgroup axioms (identity,
inverses, closure — each axiom powering exactly one property), identify
the equivalence classes as the left cosets, then show every coset has
$|H|$ elements via the bijection $h \mapsto gh$ whose injectivity is
left cancellation. Counting the partition gives
$|G| = [G:H] \cdot |H|$. Techniques: equivalence relation from a
subgroup, bijection by translation, counting a set by tiling it with
equal-sized pieces. Instantiates the principle that a subgroup induces a
partition of the group into translates of itself, so the subgroup's size
divides the whole. Introductory difficulty: every step is a one-line
verification, no actions, no quotients. Pairs with the group-action
proof of the same theorem (proof/ga-lagrange-djelovanje) as two
genuinely different arguments for one statement — this one is the
hands-on, first-encounter version.
