A first functional-equation exercise: the shift relation f(x+1) = f(x) + 2
with f(0) = 1, asking for small values, a formula f(n) = 2n+1 on the
naturals, and one value below zero. It teaches the two most basic moves of
the subject before any solving tricks appear: substituting well-chosen
concrete arguments into a functional identity, and propagating a relation
along the integers by induction. Part c) tests reading the quantifier — the
identity holds for all real x, so x = -1 is legal and pins down f(-1).

Abstract principle instantiated: a functional equation is a machine for
generating equalities, one per substitution; a recurrence-shaped identity
determines values on an orbit of the shift (here Z) but nothing off it, which
the closing remark makes explicit so beginners do not overclaim uniqueness on
all of R. Techniques: direct substitution, induction on n. Common failure
mode: asserting f(x) = 2x+1 for all real x. Difficulty 1 — the intended first
exercise after seeing the definition of a functional equation; suitable for
"easiest functional equations you have" queries.
