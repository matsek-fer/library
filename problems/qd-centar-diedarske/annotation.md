Tests hands-on quotient-group technique in a small non-abelian setting: a
normality check done by explicit conjugation (not by citing a theorem), the
construction of a coset multiplication table via representatives, and the
identification of a quotient's isomorphism type purely from element orders.
The group is the dihedral group of the square, the subgroup its center.

Techniques: the rewriting rule $sr^k = r^{-k}s$ for dihedral computations,
center computation by checking commutation with generators, normality
verified elementwise by conjugation, coset arithmetic with well-definedness
justified by normality, detecting the Klein four-group by the criterion
"every element squares to the identity, hence no element of order 4, hence
not cyclic". The solution deliberately avoids the first isomorphism
theorem — no homomorphism or kernel appears; the isomorphism type is read
off the coset table directly. Suitable for queries that exclude
first-iso-theorem machinery.

Abstract principle instantiated: quotienting by the center collapses
exactly the failure of commutativity — the coset table of $D_4/Z(D_4)$ is
symmetric although $D_4$ is non-abelian. Also illustrates that central
subgroups are normal for the trivial reason that conjugation fixes them.
Common failures: multiplying cosets without checking well-definedness;
concluding cyclicity from the group being generated "by one coset and its
powers" without computing orders; mis-applying $sr = rs$ in a dihedral
group.
