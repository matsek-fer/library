Bridge item between group actions and number theory: Fermat's little
theorem proved by letting the cyclic group $\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}$ act
on length-$p$ strings over $a$ colours by cyclic rotation (the necklace
argument). Tests whether the solver can verify an action given by an
index-shift formula, characterize the fixed points (one-element orbits
are exactly the constant strings, $a$ of them), and use Lagrange plus
orbit–stabilizer to show every orbit has size 1 or $p$ because a
stabilizer inside a group of prime order is trivial or everything.
Counting $a^p = a + mp$ over the orbit partition yields
$p \mid a^p - a$, and Euclid's lemma converts this to
$a^{p-1} \equiv 1 \pmod p$. Techniques: orbit counting, fixed-point
characterization, prime order forcing orbit sizes, class equation in
miniature. Instantiates the principle that a divisibility statement can
be proved by exhibiting a set of the right cardinality and a group
action whose orbit sizes are controlled — congruences become geometry.
Also flags where primality is used twice (orbit sizes, Euclid's lemma)
and that the composite analogue $n \mid a^n - a$ fails. Common failure:
asserting all non-fixed orbits have size $p$ without the stabilizer
argument. Croatian-language item; answers queries linking group actions
to number theory (Fermat/Euler via orbits, necklace counting).
