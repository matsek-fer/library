The central construction of the coset-action ladder: turning the coset
space $G/H$ from a passive partition into a $G$-set via left
multiplication. Tests whether the solver can (1) run a well-definedness
argument on coset representatives using the criterion $xH = yH \iff
y^{-1}x \in H$, (2) verify the two action axioms and extract bijectivity
from them, (3) prove transitivity by solving $g \cdot xH = yH$
explicitly, and (4) compute that the stabilizer of $xH$ is the conjugate
$xHx^{-1}$, so the stabilizer of the base point $eH$ is $H$ itself.
Techniques: representative-independence checks, inverse-as-witness for
bijectivity, conjugation appearing naturally as a change of base point.
Instantiates the principle that subgroups of index $n$ are exactly point
stabilizers of transitive actions on $n$ points — the dictionary between
internal data (a subgroup) and external data (an action). Common
failures: skipping well-definedness entirely, or claiming the stabilizer
of every coset is $H$ rather than a conjugate of $H$. Prerequisite for
the homomorphism $G \to \mathrm{Sym}(G/H)$ and the normal-core argument
higher up the ladder.
