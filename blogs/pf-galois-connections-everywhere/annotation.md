An expository blog presenting Galois connections as a general order-theoretic
device, with Galois theory as one instance rather than the definition.
Content ladder: the adjunction equivalence f(p) <= q iff p <= g(q) on posets
with a proof that left adjoints preserve suprema and right adjoints preserve
infima; the floor/ceiling pair as the two adjoints of the inclusion Z -> R,
including a counterexample showing floor fails to preserve suprema; the
subgroup/fixed-field antitone connection with the fundamental theorem of
Galois theory framed as the case where the connection is a bijection; the
induced closure operator g∘f, instantiated by the generated-subgroup operator
on subsets of a group (with span, generated ideal, topological closure and
deductive closure named as the same template); and the categorical upgrade —
Galois connections are adjunctions between thin categories, with a paragraph
on RAPL/LAPC: abelianization as left adjoint to Ab -> Grp preserving
coproducts, giving (G*H)^ab = G^ab ⊕ H^ab and (Z/2 * Z/3)^ab = Z/6
concretely, mirrored against floor's failure with suprema.

Abstract principle instantiated: adjoint pairs transfer questions across the
adjunction, forcing left adjoints to preserve colimits (joins) and right
adjoints limits (meets); closure operators are shadows of connections. Serves
queries about where Galois connections or adjoint-functor preservation
phenomena appear across fields — order theory, algebra, topology, logic —
beyond Galois theory itself. Checkpoints test the floor adjunction
equivalence and identifying the induced closure operator.
