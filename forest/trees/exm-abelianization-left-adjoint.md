# Abelianization: a left adjoint earning its keep

An adjunction outside all order theory, where the preservation theorem
(left adjoints preserve colimits, right adjoints preserve limits —
[[con-galois-connections-are-adjunctions]]) computes something that
would otherwise take a page.

## The adjunction

For a group $G$ let $[G, G]$ be its commutator subgroup, generated by
all $aba^{-1}b^{-1}$, and let
$$G^{\mathrm{ab}} = G / [G, G]$$
be its **abelianization**. This is a functor
$\mathrm{ab} \colon \mathbf{Grp} \to \mathbf{Ab}$. Let
$U \colon \mathbf{Ab} \to \mathbf{Grp}$ be the inclusion, which simply
forgets that a group is abelian.

Then $\mathrm{ab} \dashv U$: for every group $G$ and abelian group $A$,
$$\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{Ab}}\big(G^{\mathrm{ab}}, A\big)
  \;\cong\;
  \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{Grp}}\big(G, U A\big),$$
naturally in both. The bijection is precomposition with the quotient map
$G \to G^{\mathrm{ab}}$, and it is a bijection because any homomorphism
$\varphi \colon G \to A$ into an abelian group kills every commutator
($\varphi(aba^{-1}b^{-1}) = e$), hence factors uniquely through the
quotient by $[G,G]$.

## What it must therefore preserve

Being a **left** adjoint, $\mathrm{ab}$ preserves colimits — in
particular coproducts. The coproduct in $\mathbf{Grp}$ is the free
product $G * H$; the coproduct in $\mathbf{Ab}$ is the direct sum. So,
with no element-level computation at all,
$$(G * H)^{\mathrm{ab}} \;\cong\; G^{\mathrm{ab}} \oplus
  H^{\mathrm{ab}}.$$

A concrete reading: $\mathbb{Z}/2 * \mathbb{Z}/3$ is a large, visibly
non-abelian group (it is isomorphic to
$\mathrm{PSL}(2, \mathbb{Z})$), yet
$$\big(\mathbb{Z}/2 * \mathbb{Z}/3\big)^{\mathrm{ab}}
  \cong \mathbb{Z}/2 \oplus \mathbb{Z}/3 \cong \mathbb{Z}/6,$$
the last step because $\gcd(2,3) = 1$. One line, and the abelianization
of the modular group is read off.

## What it is under no obligation to preserve

Limits. And it does fail them, in the smallest available example.

In $\mathbf{Grp}$, the kernel of a homomorphism is a limit: $\ker
\varphi$ is the equalizer of $\varphi$ and the trivial homomorphism.
Take the sign map $\operatorname{sgn} \colon S_3 \to \mathbb{Z}/2$. Its
kernel is $A_3 \cong \mathbb{Z}/3$, which is already abelian, so
applying $\mathrm{ab}$ to the kernel gives $\mathbb{Z}/3$.

Now apply $\mathrm{ab}$ first. Since $[S_3, S_3] = A_3$, we get
$S_3^{\mathrm{ab}} \cong \mathbb{Z}/2$, and the induced map
$S_3^{\mathrm{ab}} \to \mathbb{Z}/2$ is an isomorphism — so *its* kernel
is trivial. Trivial group against $\mathbb{Z}/3$: the two answers
differ, and abelianization has not preserved this limit.

Finite products it happens to preserve, because
$[G \times H, G \times H] = [G,G] \times [H,H]$ — commutators in a
direct product are computed coordinatewise. That is a piece of luck
about products, not a consequence of the adjunction, and the kernel
example shows the general duty is absent.

## The moral

Floor keeping minima and dropping suprema
([[rem-floor-fails-suprema]]), the generated subgroup turning unions
into joins ([[exm-generated-subgroup-closure]]), and abelianization
turning free products into direct sums are one theorem in three
costumes. Knowing which side of an adjunction a construction sits on
tells you, before any computation, which of your favourite limits or
colimits it will respect and which it will shrug at.
