# Left adjoints preserve suprema, right adjoints preserve infima

Let $f \colon P \to Q$ and $g \colon Q \to P$ be a Galois connection
between posets, so $f(p) \le q \iff p \le g(q)$ for all $p \in P$,
$q \in Q$, with $f$ the lower (left) adjoint and $g$ the upper (right)
adjoint ([[def-galois-connection]]).

**Theorem.** Let $S \subseteq P$ and $T \subseteq Q$.

1. If $\sup S$ exists in $P$, then $\sup f(S)$ exists in $Q$ and
   $$f\big(\sup S\big) = \sup f(S).$$
2. If $\inf T$ exists in $Q$, then $\inf g(T)$ exists in $P$ and
   $$g\big(\inf T\big) = \inf g(T).$$

Here $f(S) = \{f(p) : p \in S\}$ and $g(T) = \{g(q) : q \in T\}$. No
completeness is assumed anywhere: the theorem says only that *existing*
bounds are carried across, and it says nothing about the other pairing —
a left adjoint has no duty toward infima, nor a right adjoint toward
suprema, and both duties really do fail in practice
([[rem-floor-fails-suprema]]).

This is the workhorse of the whole subject. Everything one proves about
a specific connection by hand — that floor commutes with minima, that
the subgroup generated by a union of subsets is the join of the
subgroups they generate, that abelianization turns a free product into a
direct sum — is this statement read in a particular poset or category
([[exm-floor-ceiling-adjunction]], [[exm-generated-subgroup-closure]],
[[con-galois-connections-are-adjunctions]]).
